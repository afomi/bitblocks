# BSV 402: paying Bitblocks in satoshis, per request

Status: slices 1, 2 and 4 built, 2026-11-23, and qart's slice B on our side (a keyed caller past its budget pays), 2026-11-27, all behind `BSV402_ENABLED` (off until Ryan sets the key and the price); the rest is design.
The build plan, and what each slice shipped, is section 7.
Audience: Ryan, who decides the key and the price, and the developer who builds the slices.
Related: `docs/STRIPE.md` (x402 in USDC), `docs/INTEROP_KIT.md` (BEEF vectors, BRC-77, the acceptance record), `docs/MERKLE_PROOFS.md`, `docs/USAGE_METERING.md`, and `BitblocksWeb.Plugs.RateLimiter`'s moduledoc (the 402 we already send).

An agent or app that has run through its free budget gets a 402 today, and can pay it in USDC through x402 (`Bitblocks.Payments.X402`).
This doc is about letting it pay the same 402 in BSV, from any BRC-100 wallet, checked against our own headers and our own node, with no facilitator in the middle.

## 1. The recommendation

**Adopt BRC-121 as is, and carry the same payment in the x402 envelope as BSV's `exact` scheme. Define nothing of our own on the wire.**

[BRC-121, Simple 402 Payments](https://github.com/bitcoin-sv/BRCs/blob/master/payments/0121.md) is the BSV Association's small 402 profile.
It takes one 402 and one paid retry.
The payment is a BRC-29 output in an Atomic BEEF, and it needs no session.
The same payment, the same fields and the same checks are what the BSV Association has proposed to the x402 Foundation as BSV's `exact` scheme, in [x402-foundation/x402#2890](https://github.com/x402-foundation/x402/pull/2890) (open, approved by one reviewer, read 2026-11-23).
So one verifier in Bitblocks serves two envelopes:

- **Native:** BRC-121's `x-bsv-*` headers, which BSV Browser, the 402 browser extensions and `@bsv/402-pay` already send ([named in BRC-121](https://github.com/bitcoin-sv/BRCs/blob/master/payments/0121.md)).
- **x402:** one more entry in the `accepts` list of the `PAYMENT-REQUIRED` offer we already make, `scheme: "exact"`, `network: "bsv:mainnet"`, beside USDC on Base, so an x402 client picks whichever rail it holds.

### Why not BRC-105 first

[BRC-105](https://github.com/bitcoin-sv/BRCs/blob/master/payments/0105.md) is the larger framework, and it is sound, but it rides on a BRC-103 mutual-auth session carried over HTTP by BRC-104.
Its reference middleware refuses to run without `req.auth.identityKey` from the auth middleware, and uses that key as the payer ([`payment-express-middleware` source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bsv-blockchain/payment-express-middleware/master/src/index.ts); the repo is archived, and development has moved to [ts-stack](https://github.com/bsv-blockchain/ts-stack)).
Bitblocks doesn't speak BRC-103 today, and building its handshake, its signed requests and its signed responses in Elixir is a protocol project of its own.
BRC-121 has the same payment underneath (BRC-29, Atomic BEEF, the `wallet payment` protocol) without that session.
BRC-105 can come later, behind the same verifier, if a caller needs a mutually authenticated session (section 8).

### Why not BRC-166 first

[BRC-166, P2PKH Payments for HTTP 402](https://github.com/bitcoin-sv/BRCs/blob/master/payments/0166.md), is x402's `exact` scheme with a plain P2PKH payment that any wallet can make.
It carries a raw transaction, not a BEEF, so there is no SPV, the origin must broadcast to learn anything, and it grants at zero confirmations by design.
It also needs a fresh address per invoice, which means server-side invoice state.
It is the right answer for wallets with no BRC-100 support, and a later slice if callers ask for it (section 7, slice 6).

### Why not our own

Three public profiles already cover this ground, by the people who write the wallets.
A fourth from us would be one more thing for a wallet to implement and for us to keep in step.
What we can add is evidence: vectors, a live mainnet server, and an Elixir verifier that doesn't sit on a BRC-100 wallet (section 8).

### What "not too clever" means here

- The wire format is someone else's, quoted, and pinned to a date and a commit.
- Every check is one we already make elsewhere: BRC-42 derivation (`Bitblocks.Interop.Brc42`), the BEEF reader and verifier (`Bitblocks.Chain.Beef.Reader`, `Bitblocks.Chain.BeefVerifier`), claim-once (`Bitblocks.Payments.X402Guard`), and our own node's `sendrawtransaction`.
- The price is a fixed number of satoshis that Ryan sets, not a live dollar conversion.
- No credit, no balance, no session, no refunds: one payment buys one request.
- Our node decides whether the transaction is valid and not already spent; we don't run script ourselves.

### Risks

- x402's BSV `exact` scheme is an open pull request; its shape can change before it merges.
  The native BRC-121 envelope doesn't depend on it, so the x402 entry ships behind its own switch and follows the PR.
- BRC-121 gives no lifecycle status; it reads as a working draft.
  We pin to the commit we build against and say so in `/start/api`.
- A paid request is served at zero confirmations.
  The loss from a double spend is one request's price, and section 5 bounds it further.
- The server holds a key that can spend what it receives (section 4, "The identity key").

## 2. The flow, end to end

Who does each step: **Bitblocks** (the server), **the client** (an agent's fetch wrapper, qart, or any app), and **the wallet** (any BRC-100 wallet: BSV Browser, BSV Desktop, a server wallet from `@bsv/wallet-toolbox`, or qart's own).

1. **The client asks.**
   `GET /about` with no key, past a shared free budget (a crawler family's, or the anonymous site's).
   Only a route the free budget opens is offered for sale (`AiCrawlers.payable?/1`, as for x402): with the sign-in gate on in prod, that is the public pages, not `/api/v1/*`, which takes a key.
2. **Bitblocks answers 402** (`BitblocksWeb.Plugs.RateLimiter`), with the terms in the headers BRC-121 names:
   - `x-bsv-sats`: the price in satoshis, set by Ryan;
   - `x-bsv-server`: the server's identity key, compressed hex;
   - and, with x402 on, the existing `PAYMENT-REQUIRED` offer, now listing USDC and BSV (section 4, "Coexistence").
   `Retry-After` still says when the free budget opens again, and `OpenCors` exposes the new headers on the open routes and allows the five request headers.
   The body (JSON, HTML or text) names the way in as `ways_in.pay_in_bsv`, only while it's on.
3. **The client makes its terms.**
   It picks a derivation prefix (random, at least 8 bytes, base64) and the time in Unix milliseconds.
   BRC-121 has the client, not the server, choose the prefix, so Bitblocks keeps no state between the 402 and the retry.
4. **The wallet pays.**
   The client calls `createAction` with one P2PKH output of `x-bsv-sats` satoshis to the key derived by BRC-42 from `x-bsv-server`, under BRC-29's protocol `[2, "3241645161d8"]` and key ID `<prefix> <base64(time)>` ([BRC-29](https://github.com/bitcoin-sv/BRCs/blob/master/payments/0029.md)), with `options.noSend` so the wallet doesn't broadcast it, and `randomizeOutputs: false` so the output index is known.
   `@bsv/402-pay` 0.3.6's client doesn't set `noSend`, so its wallet may broadcast first; our node then answers "already known", which counts as taken.
   Its client sends the key ID's suffix as the base64 of the `x-bsv-time` text, which is what we derive with.
   The wallet picks coins, adds change, signs, and returns Atomic BEEF (BRC-95): the payment and the ancestry that proves its inputs.
5. **The client retries** the identical request with:
   - `x-bsv-beef`: the Atomic BEEF, base64;
   - `x-bsv-sender`: the payer's identity key;
   - `x-bsv-nonce`: the prefix;
   - `x-bsv-time`: the milliseconds, as a decimal string;
   - `x-bsv-vout`: the output index.
   Through x402, the same five values travel as the `exact` scheme's payload in `PAYMENT-SIGNATURE`, per [x402#2890](https://github.com/x402-foundation/x402/pull/2890).
6. **Bitblocks checks, in this order, cheapest first** (section 5 says why each is there):
   1. the paid budget per IP and site-wide, before any parsing: the same two caps as x402 (`X402_PAID_PER_IP_PER_MINUTE`, `X402_PAID_SITE_PER_MINUTE`), shared by both rails; over either, a 429;
   2. all five values present and well formed, the BEEF at most 12,000 bytes (16,000 in base64; Bandit's per-header limit is raised to 16,384 to match the load balancer's 16 KB);
   3. `x-bsv-time` within 30 seconds of our clock, as BRC-121 says;
   4. the BEEF reads as Atomic BEEF, and its subject txid isn't already claimed (`bsv_payments`, below);
   5. the output at `x-bsv-vout` is P2PKH to the key we derive with our identity private key, the sender's key and the same key ID, and holds at least `x-bsv-sats` (the x402 `exact` envelope asks for exactly the price, so that envelope checks equality);
   6. every input's ancestry proves against **our own headers** (`BeefVerifier`, the code behind `POST /api/v1/beef/verify`): each BUMP root is a header we hold on the best chain.
      Headers sync to the node's tip whatever the stop height, so this works today.
   7. the txid is claimed in `bsv_payments` (insert-unique), so a second request with the same payment loses the race;
      as built, the claimed check of step 4 runs before steps 5 and 6, and an ancestry naming a height we hold no header for is `unknown_block`, apart from `unproven`, so a client can tell "not yet" from "wrong";
   8. the raw transaction goes to **our own node** (`BitcoinsvCli.sendrawtransaction/1`), which runs the scripts and refuses a double spend of its mempool; "already in the mempool" counts as accepted.
7. **Bitblocks serves** the response, with:
   - `x-bsv-payment-satoshis-paid`: what was paid (the header BRC-105 uses for the same fact);
   - `x-bsv-txid`: the payment's txid;
   - with the x402 envelope, `PAYMENT-RESPONSE` as x402 defines it.
   A refusal is the 402 again, with the terms, `x-bsv-refused: <reason>`, and under `/api/` a `payment_refused` object in the body; too large is a 413, and our node or database unavailable a 503 with `Retry-After: 5`.
   The paid request is counted in the usage meter under the payer's identity key (section 3).
8. **Later, by itself:** a minute cron (`BsvPaymentWorker`) asks our node where each accepted payment is, marks it `confirmed` once it is in a block our headers hold and `settled` six blocks deep, and marks one that never makes a block `dropped` and one whose input was spent elsewhere `conflicted` (section 3, "Following a payment").
9. **Optionally, the receipt:** `GET /api/v1/payments/:txid/receipt` returns our acceptance record (`interop-kit/payment-acceptance/v0`, `Bitblocks.Interop.PaymentAcceptance`), signed with BRC-77 by the same identity key, naming the output we took as payment.
   This is a plain route beside the protocol, not a change to it.

What the wallet never does: see our key, or broadcast.
What Bitblocks never does: sign for the payer, hold the payer's coins, or call a third party to decide.

## 3. What each side keeps

### Bitblocks

One table, `bsv_payments`, one row per accepted payment, written once and moved only forward:

| Field | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| `txid` | unique: the replay guard |
| `vout`, `satoshis` | the output we took |
| `sender_key` | the payer's identity key |
| `derivation_prefix`, `derivation_suffix` | exactly as sent: with our key, they are what spends the output (BRC-29's remittance) |
| `raw_tx` | the payment's bytes; the BEEF's ancestry is not kept once checked |
| `request_hash` | SHA-256 of method, path and query: what was bought |
| `envelope` | `brc121` or `x402` |
| `price_sats` | the price at the time, so a later price change doesn't rewrite history |
| `status` | `accepted`, `confirmed`, `settled`, `dropped`, `conflicted` |
| `block_hash`, `block_height` | once mined |
| `caller` | who paid: the payer's identity key, or `key:<id>` for a caller with an API key (slice B) |
| timestamps | `accepted_at`, `confirmed_at` |

- **Replay guard:** the unique `txid`.
  BRC-121's 30-second window stops a delayed replay; the unique row stops an immediate one, across nodes, since it lives in the database.
- **The same payment, the same request:** a retry whose `txid` is claimed and whose `request_hash` matches, inside the freshness window, is served again rather than refused, so a client whose response was lost isn't charged twice (BRC-166 does the same).
  A claimed `txid` with a different `request_hash` is refused.
- **Usage:** `Bitblocks.Usage.caller/1` learns one more kind, `{"bsv", sender_key}`, from an assign the plug sets on a paid request.
  The hourly rollups then show paid traffic beside keyed and anonymous traffic, with no second meter.
- **The receipt:** built on request from the row and signed then; nothing more to store.
- **What it never stores:** our private key, or the BEEF beyond the request.

### The client

- The derivation prefix, suffix and txid of each payment, and which request it paid for: the wallet does this through `createAction`'s labels, and qart through its own `transactions` row (`Qart.SPV.Evidence`).
- The receipt, if it wants proof that we took the payment.
- No cache of paid URLs is required: each paid request is its own payment.
  BRC-121 notes that a client shouldn't retry a refused payment automatically, since that can pay twice.

### When it goes wrong

- **Reorg:** the payment was accepted unconfirmed, so a reorg mostly changes its block.
  The worker moves `confirmed` back to `accepted` once our headers no longer hold its block on the best chain, and asks our node again at once; the node puts the transaction back in its mempool, and it is mined again.
  Nothing is clawed back from the caller.
- **Double spend:** our node refuses a payment that conflicts with one in its mempool, before we serve.
  A conflicting transaction mined elsewhere instead shows as `conflicted` (the node no longer knows the txid, and an input is spent by another).
  The request was served, so the loss is one price.
  The sender key goes on a short deny list, so its next payments are refused, and `Release.bsv402_status()` counts the keys refused.
- **Broadcast failure:** our node refuses the transaction (bad script, missing input, already spent): the claim is rolled back, and the caller gets the 402 again with the node's reason.
  Our node is unreachable: the claim is rolled back and the caller gets a 503 with `Retry-After`, never the resource, since we can't know the payment is good.
  The same BEEF can be sent again; it hasn't been broadcast.
- **Dropped:** a transaction our node no longer knows is sent to it again, once; one still unmined 24 hours (about 144 blocks) after it was accepted is marked `dropped`, and counted in the status.

### Following a payment

Built in slice 4: `Bitblocks.Payments.Bsv402.Confirmation`, run each minute by `Bitblocks.Workers.BsvPaymentWorker`.

| Status | When | Final? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `accepted` | our node took it into its mempool, and we served the request | no |
| `confirmed` | our node puts it in a block that our headers hold, verified and on the best chain; `block_hash` and `block_height` say which | no: a reorg moves it back to `accepted` |
| `settled` | confirmed, and its block 6 deep by our headers (the block and five on top) | yes |
| `dropped` | in no block 24 hours (about 144 blocks) after it was accepted, whether or not our node still holds it in its mempool | yes |
| `conflicted` | our node no longer knows it, and an input it spends is spent | yes |

- **Where our node puts it:** `getrawtransaction(txid, 1)`; a node without a full tx index knows a mined transaction only by its unspent outputs, so a "not found" is asked again as `gettxoutproof([txid])`, whose first 80 bytes are the block's header.
  Either way the block counts only once our own headers hold it; the sync needn't have stored its transactions.
  Neither knowing it, `gettxout` on each input says whether one is spent (`conflicted`); if none is, the transaction goes to our node once more (`rebroadcast_at`) and waits for the deadline.
  No other source is asked, WhatsOnChain included.
- **Depth:** `settle_depth` 6, `drop_after_hours` 24 and `deny_hours` 168 are config (`config :bitblocks, Bitblocks.Payments.Bsv402`).
  Six blocks is the common reading of a settled payment, and a reorg deeper than that would need a person anyway, so a `settled` row is not reopened.
- **Bounded:** at most 100 rows a run, the most overdue first; an `accepted` row is asked again after 1, 2, 4 and 8 minutes, then every 15; a `confirmed` row needs no node call, since its depth comes from our headers.
  A node that doesn't answer leaves the row as it was.
  The job runs on the `default` lane at a low priority, unique across nodes, and only on a writer (`NodeRole`), since a reader runs no cron.
- **The deny list:** a sender key with a payment `dropped` or `conflicted` within the last `deny_hours` is refused as `denied`, a 402, after the claimed check (so a lost response is still served again) and before the derivation.
  The list is those rows (`closed_at`, indexed by `sender_key`); nothing else is stored.
  Like `unavailable`, it hangs on our database's state, so it has no vector in `/interop/bsv402/v0.json`.
- **Rows kept:** a dropped or conflicted row keeps its bytes, its remittance and `reason` in plain words.

## 4. Seams

| Seam | What it decides | Owner |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The price | satoshis per request, by endpoint class | bitblocks, value set by Ryan |
| The verifier | every check in step 6 | bitblocks (`Bitblocks.Payments.Bsv402`, new) |
| Headers | whether a BUMP root is a real block | bitblocks (its header sync, already built) |
| The broadcaster | whether the tx is valid and unspent | bitblocks (its own node); ARC later as a second sender, not a judge |
| The identity key | ECDH for derivation, signing receipts | bitblocks holds it; Ryan creates it |
| Spending what was received | sweeping outputs to cold storage | to decide (below) |
| The client | building and sending the payment | qart, or any BRC-100 wallet and `@bsv/402-pay` |
| The envelope | native headers or x402 | bitblocks, one verifier, two parsers |
| Coexistence with x402 USDC | one 402, two rails | bitblocks (`RateLimiter`) |

### The price

A fixed `BSV402_PRICE_SATS`, read from the environment, set by Ryan.
A dollar peg, from the cached BSV price we already serve, comes later if wanted, with a floor and a ceiling so a bad price feed can't make a request free or absurd.
[x402#2890](https://github.com/x402-foundation/x402/pull/2890) converts dollars through WhatsOnChain's rate; we'd rather not let another site's number set our price.

### The identity key

The server needs its identity *private* key at request time: BRC-42 derivation is ECDH, and the payee's side of it takes the payee's private key (`Brc42.private_child/3`).
So the key that checks a payment is the key that can spend it.

- Make it a dedicated key, not the statement-signing key (`STATEMENT_SIGNING_KEY`) and not anyone's wallet root: a leak then costs the unswept balance and nothing else.
- Hold it where the other prod secrets are, the AWS Secrets Manager secret that `.github/scripts/deploy.sh` writes on deploy, as `BSV402_IDENTITY_KEY`, never in the repo, never printed (`Release.bsv402_status()` names it and says whether it's set).
- Publish its public half at `/.well-known/` and on `/start/api`, so a client can pin it.
- Rotate by publishing the new key beside the old for a day, then retiring the old; outputs to the old key stay spendable from the remittance rows.

### Spending what was received

Every row in `bsv_payments` plus the identity key is what it takes to spend an output.
Two ways, for Ryan to choose:

1. **Bitblocks sweeps its own revenue:** a dry-run-first `Release.bsv402_sweep/1` that pays everything above a threshold to a cold destination Ryan names.
   One key, one place, and a narrow exception to "bitblocks reads, qart signs".
2. **qart sweeps:** bitblocks exports the remittance rows, and qart signs the sweep in its own key custody.
   This keeps the boundary, but puts the identity key in two places.

The first slices don't need either: the balance is small and grows only with use, and the status page shows it.
Recommended: the first, once the balance passes a threshold Ryan sets.

### Coexistence with x402 USDC

- One 402 carries both: the `x-bsv-*` headers for BSV wallets, and `PAYMENT-REQUIRED` with both `accepts` entries for x402 clients.
- A retry carries one: `x-bsv-beef` goes to the BSV verifier, and `PAYMENT-SIGNATURE` goes to the scheme it names (`exact` on `bsv:mainnet` to ours, `exact` on Base to x402_ex).
- Both share the paid budgets and the claim-once discipline, and both are served as the same anonymous caller with a paid assign.
- Neither waits for the other: USDC on mainnet is still sandbox-only here (`docs/STRIPE.md`, "Later: x402 live mode").

## 5. Attack vectors

| Attack | Mitigation | Bounded by the 402 budget already? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Replay**: the same payment for a second request | unique `txid` claim, in the database; 30-second window | no, the claim does it |
| **Cross-request replay**: a captured payment used for another URL inside the window | the claim makes it single-use; TLS keeps it from being captured | no |
| **Underpayment** | the output must hold at least the price (exactly, under x402 `exact`); only the one named output counts, change ignored | no |
| **Paying someone else**: an output that isn't ours | we derive the key ourselves from our private key and the sender's key; a mismatch is refused | no |
| **Forged BEEF**: invented parents or merkle paths | each BUMP root must be a header we hold on the best chain; the node then refuses inputs that don't exist | no |
| **Forged signatures**: a well-formed tx spending real outputs it can't unlock | our node runs the scripts before we serve | no |
| **Zero-conf double spend** | our node's mempool refuses a conflict it has seen; a mined conflict loses one price and denies the sender key; a price high enough to matter can wait for a confirmation | partly: the paid budget caps how many a payer can try per minute |
| **Fee sniping and low fees**: a payment that pays too little fee to be mined | our node's minimum relay fee refuses it at broadcast; one that is accepted and never mined is `dropped` and its sender denied | partly |
| **Pricing manipulation** | a fixed price in satoshis from our own config; no outside feed sets it; the price is in each row | no |
| **Identity key compromise** | a dedicated key; balance swept above a threshold; rotation as above; the key never logged | no |
| **DoS by costly verification**: large BEEFs, deep ancestry | the paid budget per IP and per sender key runs before any parsing; a byte cap on the header; at most a few dozen ancestors; cheap checks first, and the node call last, once | yes: the per-IP and per-family paid budgets exist for x402 today and extend to this |
| **Header size**: a BEEF too big for a header | Bandit's and the load balancer's header limits refuse it; we set our cap below both, and say so in the 402 body | n/a |
| **Clock skew games** | ±30 s on our clock; the key ID binds the time, so it can't be changed without a new payment | no |
| **Wrong network**: a testnet payment to mainnet | headers decide: a testnet BUMP root isn't on our chain; x402 names `bsv:mainnet` and refuses `bip122:` | no |

The budget bounds attempts, not their success: every row above "DoS" needs its own check, and has one.

## 6. Test vectors

Published as [`/interop/bsv402/v0.json`](https://bitblocks.app/interop/bsv402/v0.json), beside `/interop/vectors/v1.json`, built by `scripts/bsv402_vectors_v0.exs` with outcomes written by hand, and run by `test/bitblocks/payments/bsv402_test.exs` so the file can't drift from the code (the same discipline as `docs/INTEROP_KIT.md`, "The vectors").
Built in slice 1, with the BRC-121 cases below; the `x402-*` cases come with slice 5, and `live-wallet` once captured.
v0 until a real wallet's payment is in it; then v1.
Within v0, vectors are only added: the `keyed` cases came on 2026-11-27 and `keyed-unbudgeted` on 2026-11-28 (each listed in the file's `changes`), and the cases before them are unchanged.

Shape:

```json
{
  "version": "bsv402/v0",
  "basis": {"brc121": "<commit>", "brc29": "<commit>", "x402_bsv_exact": "<PR head commit>"},
  "server": {"identity_private_key": "<test key, hex>", "identity_key": "<hex>"},
  "headers": [{"height": 3100001, "synthetic": true, "merkleroot": "<hex>", "tx_count": 1}],
  "now_ms": 0,
  "price_sats": 100,
  "vectors": [
    {
      "id": "pays-exact",
      "envelope": "brc121",
      "request": {"method": "GET", "path": "/api/v1/blocks/latest"},
      "payment": {"x-bsv-beef": "…", "x-bsv-sender": "…", "x-bsv-nonce": "…", "x-bsv-time": "…", "x-bsv-vout": "0"},
      "claimed": [],
      "node": "accept",
      "expected": {"status": 200, "outcome": "accepted", "reason": null}
    }
  ]
}
```

The keys are test keys made for the file from fixed labels, and `price_sats` is the file's own number, not our price.
The headers are made up, as `/interop/vectors/v1.json`'s synthetic ones are: each is a one-tx block holding the vector's coin, so a reader relies on its `merkleroot` alone.
The transactions are unsigned, so `node` says what the stubbed node answers.
`claimed` lists `{txid, request}` pairs to treat as already taken.
`claimed` lists txids to treat as already taken, `node` what the stubbed node answers (`accept`, `reject:<reason>`, `unreachable`, `already_known`), and `expected.reason` our name for the refusal.

| id | Case | Expected |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `pays-exact` | one output of exactly the price, ancestry proven | 200, accepted |
| `pays-over` | more than the price | 200 native; 402 `amount_mismatch` under x402 `exact` |
| `pays-under` | one sat short | 402, `underpaid` |
| `wrong-key` | P2PKH to a key not derived for us | 402, `not_our_output` |
| `wrong-suffix` | the time header differs from the key ID's time | 402, `not_our_output` |
| `wrong-vout` | `x-bsv-vout` names the change | 402, `not_our_output` |
| `stale` | time 31 s old | 402, `stale` |
| `future` | time 31 s ahead | 402, `stale` |
| `replay` | a txid in `claimed`, other request | 402, `replayed` |
| `replay-same-request` | a txid in `claimed`, same request, in the window | 200, `reserved` (served again, no new row) |
| `unknown-root` | a BUMP whose root isn't the header's at its height | 402, `unproven` |
| `unknown-block` | a BUMP at a height with no header | 402, `unknown_block` |
| `not-atomic` | a BEEF V1 without the Atomic prefix | 402, `malformed` |
| `bad-beef` | truncated bytes | 402, `malformed` |
| `missing-header` | no `x-bsv-sender` | 402, `incomplete` |
| `node-rejects` | `node: reject:bad-txns-inputs-spent` | 402, `broadcast_rejected` |
| `node-down` | `node: unreachable` | 503, `broadcast_unavailable` |
| `node-knows-it` | `node: already_known` | 200, accepted |
| `oversize` | a BEEF over the cap | 413, `too_large` |
| `x402-pays` | the `pays-exact` payment in `PAYMENT-SIGNATURE` | 200, accepted, `PAYMENT-RESPONSE` |
| `x402-bip122` | the same with `network: "bip122:…"` | 402, `wrong_network` |
| `live-wallet` | bytes from a real BRC-100 wallet on mainnet, our test key as server | 200, accepted (added once captured) |

Each case pins one rule.

The `keyed` array runs through the HTTP front rather than the verifier alone, for a caller holding an API key (qart's server, slice B).
Each names `api_key` (a token made from a label, whether it is registered, its `requests_per_minute`, and how many requests it has `spent` this minute), the `request_headers` (the key), the payment or null, and `expected` as a status, exact `headers`, `headers_present`, `headers_absent`, and fields the JSON `body` must hold.
`test/bitblocks_web/plugs/rate_limiter_bsv402_keyed_test.exs` runs each through the plug.

| id | Case | Expected |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `keyed-under-budget` | a key within its budget, no payment | 200, served |
| `keyed-over-budget` | a key past its budget, no payment | 402 with `x-bsv-sats`, `x-bsv-server`, `Retry-After`; body `error: api_key_rate_limited` |
| `keyed-pays` | a key past its budget, with a payment | 200, accepted, `x-bsv-payment-satoshis-paid` and `x-bsv-txid` |
| `keyed-replay` | as `keyed-pays`, its txid already bought another request | 402, `x-bsv-refused: replayed`, the terms again |
| `keyed-bad-key` | an unknown key carrying a payment | 401, no terms; the payment isn't read |
| `keyed-unbudgeted` | a key holding the `unbudgeted` scope (`api_key.scopes`, on this vector only), 2 spent against `requests_per_minute: 1` | 200, served; no `x-bsv-*`, no `x-ratelimit-*`, no `Retry-After` |

The derivation vectors should also run against `@bsv/sdk`, so a reader in another language can check our reading of BRC-29 rather than trust it.
Built: `test/support/fixtures/brc/bsv402_brc29_sdk.json` holds `@bsv/sdk` 4.1.2's derived keys for the file's keys, nonce and times, and the test derives the same.

## 7. The build plan

Each slice ships alone, behind `BSV402_ENABLED`, off by default, and each has its test.

### Bitblocks (server)

1. **The verifier, no HTTP.** Built.

   Test: `test/bitblocks/payments/bsv402_test.exs` runs every vector in section 6 except the x402 and live-wallet ones; derivation checked against `@bsv/sdk`'s output, captured once as a fixture.
2. **The native 402 and retry.** Built.
   The `x-bsv-*` terms on the existing 402 beside x402's offer, the paid retry through `RateLimiter` (the shared paid caps), our node's `sendrawtransaction`, the receipt headers, CORS, the usage caller `bsv:<sender key>`, `/pricing#crawlers-bsv` (shown only while on), `Release.bsv402_status()` and a line in `Release.status()`.
   Test: `test/bitblocks_web/plugs/rate_limiter_bsv402_test.exs`, a 402 then a paid retry served, each refusal, the 503, the 413, the cap, and the same-request re-serve.
3. **The live run.** 🖐

4. **Confirmation.** Built.

5. **The x402 envelope.**
   `exact` on `bsv:mainnet` in `accepts` and in `PAYMENT-SIGNATURE`, following [x402#2890](https://github.com/x402-foundation/x402/pull/2890) at a pinned commit, behind `BSV402_X402_ENABLED`.
   Test: the `x402-*` vectors, through the plug.
6. **Later, each on demand:** the signed receipt route; a dollar-pegged price; the sweep; BRC-166's plain P2PKH for wallets without BRC-100; BRC-105 for authenticated sessions.

### qart (client)

qart is unchanged by this doc; its asks are in `~/workspace/qart/.claude/inbox/from-bitblocks-2026-11-23-bsv-402-client.md`.

- **A. The browser payment.**
  `assets/js/pay/sats_action.js` already builds a `createAction` with `noSend` and returns BEEF.
  It needs a BRC-29 output (`[2, "3241645161d8"]`, counterparty the server key) rather than a locking script handed to it, `randomizeOutputs: false`, and Atomic BEEF base64 into the five headers.
  `@bsv/402-pay`'s client may do all of this already; qart should check before writing its own.
  Test: a JS test against slice 1's vectors, with a stubbed wallet.
- **B. qart's server as a paying client.**
  qart calls Bitblocks with an API key today (`Qart.Bitblocks.Keys`); a paid fallback lets a qart instance carry on past its key's budget, paying from its operational wallet.
  This is the one place qart signs for itself, so it rides on its operational key decisions ([qart#182](https://github.com/afomi/qart/issues/182)).
  Test: an ExUnit test against a stubbed Bitblocks returning the 402.
  Tracked as [qart#383](https://github.com/afomi/qart/issues/383).
  **Bitblocks' side built, 2026-11-27**, behind `BSV402_ENABLED`, as decided with qart:
  - A keyed caller past its key's budget on a route under `/api/v1` the key opens gets the 402 with BRC-121's terms instead of the 429, whenever BSV 402 is on (x402's offer too, when that's on); with BSV 402 off, the 429 as before.
  - A paid retry carries the same key and the five `x-bsv-*` headers, goes through the same paid caps (`paid:ip`, `paid:site`) and `Bsv402.verify/3` (claimed once, our node, the deny list), and is served with the receipt headers.
  - A paid request sits outside the key's budget: it isn't counted against the free allowance.
    It is metered under the key, and its `bsv_payments` row's `caller` is `key:<id>`, so the key's record holds the payment's txid.
  - A bad or revoked key is still a 401, never a 402; an anonymous caller is unchanged.
  - A key holding the `unbudgeted` scope never sees the 402, nor a 429 (2026-11-28, Ryan: "qart's key should not consume bitblocks budget").
    It has no budget: it isn't counted, is never offered a payment, and its responses carry no `x-ratelimit-*` headers.
    It is still metered under the key.
    The scope is granted only by name (`Release.grant_api_key_scope(id, "unbudgeted")`), and grants nothing to a revoked key, which is still a 401.
  - Vectors: the `keyed` cases in section 6, for qart's stub.
  Test: `test/bitblocks_web/plugs/rate_limiter_bsv402_keyed_test.exs`.
- **C. The live run.** 🖐
  Slice 3 above, with qart's wallet connection as the client.
- **D. Optional:** read the receipt route, and check its BRC-77 signature with the key qart pins.

What qart already has, read from its code on 2026-11-23: `createAction` requests (`Qart.Tx.Action`, `assets/js/pay/sats_action.js`, tested), a strict BEEF reader (`Qart.SPV.Beef`, `test/qart/spv/beef_test.exs`), SPV evidence storage (`Qart.SPV.Evidence`), paymail and PSBT, and an ARC broadcaster held to conformance vectors (`Qart.BSV.Arc`, no live endpoint verified).
What it doesn't have: BRC-29 derivation, `internalizeAction` on its server, BRC-103/104, or any 402 client.
qart's own `docs/BRC100.md` says its wallet paths haven't yet been run against a shipped wallet.

## 8. What we'd be first at, and what we'd give back

As far as we could find on 2026-11-23:

- **One 402 that offers both rails:** USDC on Base and BSV, in the same `accepts` list, served by one site.
- **An indexer that sells itself in satoshis**, verifying the payment against its own headers and its own node, with no wallet service and no facilitator in the loop.
- **An Elixir verifier**; the published implementations are TypeScript and Go (`@bsv/402-pay`, `go-402-pay`, named in [BRC-121](https://github.com/bitcoin-sv/BRCs/blob/master/payments/0121.md)) and Ruby ([`x402-rack`](https://sgbett.github.io/x402-rack/ecosystem/)).

What we'd contribute:

- **The vectors file** (section 6), offered to the BRCs repo with BRC-121, since BRC-121 has none.
- **Notes on BRC-121's gaps**, as a discussion or pull request rather than a new BRC:
  - BRC-121 doesn't cap the size of `x-bsv-beef`, and a header has hard limits; [BRC-118](https://github.com/bitcoin-sv/BRCs/blob/master/payments/0118.md) moves BRC-105's payment into a multipart body for this reason, and BRC-121 could say the same or name a cap;
  - it doesn't bind the payment to the request; the claim-once row does it for us, and the spec could say a server should;
  - it leaves the overpayment rule open, where x402's `exact` asks for equality.
- **A short BRC only if the review finds a gap no existing one covers**, for example a server's identity key published at a well-known path; checked against the open BRCs first.
- **A live mainnet endpoint** others can test their clients against, at a price Ryan sets.
